# Vitae Lab — Customer Psychology Brief for the Label Redesign

**Reviewed:** front-panel concept board "Mineral Atelier / A quieter kind of premium" — five SKUs, cream labels, ribbed chrome caps, pastel concentric arcs, placeholder dose text.

**Customer:** 35–60, already paying a practitioner. The label never has to *sell*. Its jobs: don't break the chain of trust the practitioner started, and make a daily act effortless. Mechanisms: processing fluency, signalling theory, pharma-vs-lifestyle category codes, inconspicuous consumption (Berger & Ward 2010), implementation intentions (Gollwitzer).

**Keep from the concept:** bone/cream stock, restraint, the single thin rule, a small wordmark.

## 1. First three seconds in the hand

| Feeling | Cue that produces it | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Trust | Numbers present: dose, serving count, lot, best-by; tabular figures; high-contrast type | Verifiability; pharma code |
| Competence | One strict grid, two sizes of secondary copy, correct units ("5 g", "2,000 IU"), no draft text | Fluency → competence inference |
| Calm | Negative space, one desaturated accent under ~15% of panel, matte soft-touch stock | Low-arousal palette |
| Exclusivity | Small, well-cut wordmark; no barcode, badge, flavour burst or price on the front | Inconspicuous consumption |
| "My practitioner chose this" | A dispensing line ("Dispensed by [Practice]" or a printed "Protocol:" field) | Source-credibility transfer |

Keep that cue discreet (practice name only, never "hormone" or "peptide"): the counter is semi-public, and the wanted signal is "considered", not "medicalised".

**What undermines them now**

- Placeholder copy ("Quantity & supplier text pending") sits in the third hierarchy slot, exactly where the dose belongs. Draft text reads as "nobody decided" — low competence.
- Concentric pastel arcs are a spa/skincare/tea code. Five pastels make the range read as *flavours*, and the arcs run under text, cutting contrast and fluency.
- Logo is small *and* fussy: a DNA-helix monogram is a biotech cliché and the wordmark looks like a default serif. Small is fine for quiet luxury; fussy is not.
- Zero verifiable content; trust is being asked to come entirely from aesthetics.
- Ribbed chrome cap and silver neck band are a cosmetics code, and the cap is the largest colour mass on the object.
- Two container heights with different label scale make one range read as two.
- "Atelier" pulls toward fashion/craft, away from clinical.

## 2. The adherence job

The bottle is the cue in a cue–routine–reward loop; the label states the *when* so the object carries the implementation intention ("when I make coffee, I take this"). Uncertainty ("did I take it? how much?") is the adherence killer.

- A fixed **dose band** in the same grid position on all five: `1 scoop (5 g) · Daily` / `2 capsules · Evening` / `1 softgel · With breakfast`. Same position every time means spatial memory, not reading.
- Time of day as a word, optionally a small sun/moon glyph; never a clock icon (medical-device code).
- Serving count on the same line ("60 servings"): pre-empts running out, the most common silent lapse.
- Morning vs evening: the PM product (magnesium) gets the darkest accent and a dark cap; AM products stay light.
- Scoop: "scoop = 5 g" on the front; one scoop size per SKU; a level-fill line on the handle.
- Clutter rule: the dose band is one line. If a front element is not brand, product, form, dose or when, it moves to the side.

## 3. Trust signals, ranked by impact (tiny brand, no trials)

1. **Practitioner-dispensed cue** — stronger than a trial for this client. Front, one quiet line.
2. **Lot number + best-by** — verifiability and legal hygiene; absence is the fastest trust-breaker for a literate buyer. Back/base, monospace, never a sticker over the label.
3. **Form transparency** — "magnesium bisglycinate chelate, 200 mg elemental", "micronised creatine monohydrate". Specificity heuristic: precise claims are believed, vague ones discounted. Form on front, amounts on back.
4. **QR to the certificate of analysis for *that* lot** — back. Mention on the front only if a per-lot CoA exists; a dead link costs more than no link.
5. **Third-party testing** — only if done; name the lab and the tests (identity, heavy metals, microbial). "Lab tested" with no object reads as evasion.
6. **Manufactured in a GMP-registered facility, [country]** — back; moderate, higher for EU/UK/AU clients.

No invented seals: this audience spots non-standard marks and discounts the whole label when it finds one.

## 4. Colour and form

| SKU | Mental model | Accent | Risk to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creatine | strength, mineral, neutral | graphite / slate | current mint reads toothpaste |
| Whey Vanilla | clean, dairy | bone with a pale straw rule | cream-brown = ice cream |
| Magnesium | evening, calm | deep indigo or muted plum | pale lavender = sleep tea |
| D3 | daylight, oil | muted ochre / amber | yellow-orange = candy; peach = skincare |
| Collagen | warmth, tissue | terracotta / clay | blush pink = beauty aisle, alienates the male majority |

Candy = saturation + roundness + gloss; pharmacy-generic = no colour, Helvetica on pure white. Between them: desaturated accents at low area on a matte substrate.

Containers: matte black HDPE = gym; chrome and gloss = cosmetics; white with a bright cap = pharmacy generic. Recommended: matte bone or warm-grey powder tubs with a same-colour cap (monolithic = premium); amber glass or matte white with a dark cap for capsules and softgels (apothecary code, plus genuine light protection for D3). Weight in the hand reads as importance (Jostmann et al. 2009): no thin-walled tubs, and size to 30/60 servings so nothing is half-empty on day one.

## 5. Copy tone

**Front panel**
1. Creatine Monohydrate · Micronised · 1 scoop (5 g) · Daily · 60 servings
2. Magnesium Bisglycinate · 200 mg elemental per 2 capsules · Evening
3. Vitamin D3 · 2,000 IU per softgel · With a meal
4. Dispensed as part of your protocol.
5. Lot and certificate of analysis on reverse.

**Side panel**
1. Each scoop provides 5 g creatine monohydrate. Nothing else.
2. Take as your practitioner has set; the serving shown is the standard, not necessarily yours.
3. Scan for this lot's certificate of analysis.
4. Store below 25 °C with the lid closed. Powder may compact; this is normal.
5. Manufactured in a GMP-registered facility in [country]. Lot [__] · Best before [__].

**Ban:** "clinically proven"; "pharmaceutical grade" (unless a pharmacopoeia grade is documented); "supports immunity / boosts energy"; "detox / cleanse"; "premium / ultra / pro / max". No exclamation marks anywhere.

## 6. Ten perception tests the final design must pass

1. Arm's-length test: a stranger names brand, product, form and dose, in that order, in under three seconds.
2. Blur test: at 50% blur the five SKUs are still distinct and the evening product is the darkest.
3. Draft test: no element could be mistaken for a placeholder or template.
4. Thumb test: dose and "when" stay visible while the tub is held normally.
5. Counter test: next to an espresso machine it reads "considered", not "gym" and not "ill".
6. Sceptic test: lot, best-by and a route to a CoA found within ten seconds, unprompted.
7. Honesty test: every claim on the label is backed by a document the practice can hand over.
8. Practitioner test: it looks like something a clinician hands you, not something bought in an airport.
9. Gym-bag test: still legible and clean after a week of scuffing; no gloss glare.
10. Family test: none of the five reads as beauty-aisle or bro-supplement, and all five read as one range.
