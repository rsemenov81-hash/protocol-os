# Vitae Lab — Compliance & Print Brief

Scope: five private-label SKUs, US market, 21 CFR 101 / DSHEA / FALCPA; CGMP (21 CFR 111) sits with the contract manufacturer (CM). Reviewed against the front-panel concept board (no dielines).
Legend: **REQ** legally required · **REC** standard practice · **OPT** optional · **VERIFY** confirm with CM or regulatory consultant. Citations only where I am confident.

## 1. Per-product

**All SKUs, REQ:** Other Ingredients in descending weight order (21 CFR 101.4); "Distributed by: [legal entity], City, ST ZIP" (21 CFR 101.5 — "Distributed by"/"Manufactured for", via a separate LLC/DBA rather than the clinic's name); a domestic address or phone number for serious adverse-event reports (21 USC 379aa-1; REC wording "To report a serious adverse event, contact …"); lot/batch number (CM must assign one under Part 111 — reserve space). Expiration/best-by is OPT, but if printed the CM must hold stability data.
**All SKUs, REC:** "Keep out of reach of children"; "Do not use if seal under cap is broken" (only with an induction seal); "Store in a cool, dry place"; pregnancy/medication consult line; Suggested Use matching the facts-panel serving size.

**Whey trade-off.** Protein powder may lawfully be a conventional food (Nutrition Facts; CM under 21 CFR 117; no FDA disclaimer; may be called a shake or snack) or a dietary supplement (Supplement Facts; "Dietary Supplement" in the identity; cannot be represented as a meal replacement or conventional food; CM under Part 111; adverse-event contact; matches the other four). Recommendation: Nutrition Facts if it will ever be marketed as a shake or meal, otherwise Supplement Facts. VERIFY.

**Notes.** (a) Characterizing flavor, 21 CFR 101.22(i): "Vanilla" must be at least half the height of the product name; if not wholly vanilla-derived it must read "natural vanilla flavor", "with other natural flavors" or "artificially flavored" — get the flavor spec. (b) "X g protein" on the front is a protein claim, so the facts panel needs a PDCAAS-corrected %DV (ask the CM). Collagen's PDCAAS is ~0: list collagen peptides as the dietary ingredient and never declare "protein" (VERIFY). (c) Confirm the magnesium is not "buffered" with magnesium oxide; if so, declare both forms. (d) Structure/function claims ("supports strength") are OPT; each triggers the disclaimer "*This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease." — boldface, adjacent or asterisk-linked, boxed when not adjacent, on every panel with a claim (21 CFR 101.93) — plus FDA notification within 30 days of marketing and substantiation on file. Disease claims ("treats deficiency") are never allowed. Recommendation: claim-free labels; the spec line is the premium cue.

## 2. Front panel (PDP) minimums

Mandatory on the PDP: (1) statement of identity — bold, parallel to the base, sized in reasonable relation to the most prominent type (21 CFR 101.3); (2) net quantity (21 CFR 101.7/101.105). Everything else sits on the information panel (immediately right of the PDP): facts panel → Other Ingredients → Contains → Distributed by/adverse-event contact, contiguous, no intervening material (FDA counts the UPC as intervening — place it after the block).

**Net quantity (REQ):** bottom 30% of the PDP, parallel to the base; metric and US ("NET WT 300 g (10.6 oz)"); capsules/softgels by count ("120 Capsules"); clear space ≥ letter height above/below and 2× the width of "N" left/right. Minimum type by PDP area: ≤5 sq in 1/16" (1.6 mm); 5–25 sq in 1/8" (3.2 mm); 25–100 sq in 3/16" (4.8 mm); >100 sq in 1/4" (6.4 mm). Cylinder PDP area = 40% × height × circumference (21 CFR 101.1): a 100 × 110 mm jar ≈ 21 sq in and a 60 × 100 mm bottle ≈ 12 sq in, so both need 3.2 mm numerals (≈ 12–13 pt). Recompute from the CM's drawings (VERIFY whether neck/cap height counts; be conservative).

**Other required text (REQ):** ≥ 1/16" (1.6 mm) on the lowercase "o" (21 CFR 101.2(c)) — 8.5–10 pt in practice; the concept's serif needs the upper end. Supplement Facts: ≥ 6 pt with 1 pt leading, title larger than all other type, rules ≥ 0.25 pt (21 CFR 101.36 — VERIFY clause); design at 7–8 pt.

**Concept is missing:** "Dietary Supplement" on every SKU; net quantity; servings/spec line; vitamin D amount; collagen source; vanilla qualifier and size; a bold identity line ("Powder/Capsules" is small and light); the entire information panel (facts, Other Ingredients, Contains: Milk, Distributed by, adverse-event contact, lot/date window, UPC, cautions).

## 3. Print production

- **Substrate/adhesive.** Powders get scooped in kitchens and gym bags; paper wicks and scuffs. All SKUs: white BOPP 50–60 µm, permanent acrylic adhesive rated for low-surface-energy HDPE/PP (ask the CM for the resin and whether jars are corona/flame treated; PET is easier).
- **Finish.** Matte overlaminate (~25 µm BOPP) on powders for moisture, whey fat and abrasion; UV matte varnish acceptable on capsule bottles. Avoid soft-touch.
- **Wrap geometry.** The visible front is roughly one third of the circumference (FDA counts 40% as PDP). A 100 mm jar = 314 mm around → front ≈ 100–125 mm; lay out front / info panel / back as thirds. Seam: 2–3 mm butt gap or 3–5 mm overlap (film only). Never run the facts panel or barcode across the seam or a shoulder taper; label height = straight label panel minus 2 mm top and bottom.
- **Bleed/safe area.** 1.5–3 mm bleed (printer specifies), 3 mm safe area, 5 mm for barcode and legal text; ±1 mm / ~1° application tolerance, so keep rules parallel to the label edge.
- **Colour.** CMYK + at most one spot (brand green Pantone) or one foil (the gold rule). Pale pastels and fine contour art (≥ 0.25 pt positive, 0.5 pt reversed) drift between proof and press — approve on a press proof on the final substrate. Five short runs → HP Indigo digital; keep art press-agnostic. Legal text in dark green or black, never pastel or reversed small; 5 pt positive / 6 pt reversed minimum anywhere.
- **Lot/date.** Reserve a blank, light, unfoiled window ~15 × 45 mm on the lower back, oriented along the container's travel direction, for the CM's coder (CIJ, thermal-transfer or laser each have placement limits). Pre-print "LOT"/"BEST BY" captions only once the CM confirms format.
- **Barcode.** GS1-licensed UPC-A, ≥ 80% (≈ 30 × 21 mm), dark on white, quiet zones intact, ladder orientation (bars running around the circumference) on small-diameter bottles. OPT QR (≥ 15 mm) to a CoA page — FDA treats linked web content as labeling: keep it claim-free and never link to the clinic's hormone/peptide pages.
- **Ask the CM/printer for:** container drawings (diameter, label-panel height, shoulder); dieline with seam type and wind direction; closure/induction-seal spec; scoop size and inclusion; desiccant; coding method and window; press type and ICC profile; substrate/laminate samples; wet/scuff test on a finished container; GS1 prefix ownership; retailer/Amazon barcode rules.

## 4. Pre-print checklist

1. "Dietary Supplement" identity on every PDP — bold, parallel to the base.
2. Net quantity: bottom 30%, metric + US, size computed from final PDP area.
3. Facts-panel type decided per SKU; serving size matches Suggested Use and the real scoop.
4. Every amount, form, %DV and Other Ingredient matches the CM's final formula sheet and CoA.
5. Vitamin D in mcg (IU optional); magnesium as elemental amount with the exact salt.
6. Allergen line checked against the full spec (milk, soy lecithin, fish gelatin, marine species).
7. Vanilla designation and type size match the flavor spec.
8. Distributor entity, city/state/ZIP, domestic phone or address for adverse events.
9. Claims audit across label, website and QR pages: no disease claims; any S/F claim has disclaimer, substantiation, 30-day notification.
10. Cautions and storage present; seal wording matches an actual induction seal.
11. Lot/best-by window approved by the CM; any expiry backed by stability data.
12. UPC: GS1-licensed, ≥ 80%, correct orientation, scanned from a physical proof.
13. Legal text ≥ 1/16" lowercase o; facts panel ≥ 6 pt; nothing reversed or pastel.
14. Dieline check: nothing required within 3 mm of an edge, across the seam or on a taper; test-apply on a real container.
15. Press proof on final substrate and laminate signed off; regulatory consultant signs the final PDF before release.

## 5. Risks in the current concept

- **"Vitae Lab" + DNA-helix mark + clinical minimalism.** "Lab" is not prohibited and is common in the category, but paired with "clinical", "pharmaceutical grade", Rx-style type or the clinic as named distributor it implies drug or physician-endorsed status (misbranding and FTC exposure). Keep the name; drop clinical copy; distribute through a separate entity; never mention peptide/hormone protocols, "patients" or "protocol" on labels or linked pages.
- **Already flagged as missing in §2, each a misbranding risk on its own:** "Vitamin D3" with no mcg/IU (the dose is the product — settle it against the UL); "Collagen" without bovine/marine source (allergen law and kosher/halal/vegetarian positioning); "Whey isolate" with no "Contains: Milk"; "Vanilla" unqualified and under the half-height rule; no "Dietary Supplement" anywhere (the most common FDA supplement citation); net quantity "pending" even though it owns the lower third of the front.
- **Pastel, low-contrast type and hairlines.** Legibility and press-tolerance risk for legal text.
- **Prop 65.** Collagen, whey and mineral products regularly exceed California's 0.5 µg/day lead safe harbour; get per-lot heavy-metal results and decide on a warning (label or online short-form) before launch — VERIFY.
- **"Made in USA".** Only if all-or-virtually-all US origin; creatine and whey are often imported — qualify or omit.
